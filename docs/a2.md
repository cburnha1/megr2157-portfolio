# A2 – Truss Stress Analysis

## Objective
Design a lightweight planar truss and connecting pins using standard material properties to safely support a specified load. I selected applied force magnitudes of 30 kN (+30 kN at Point C and -30 kN at Point D), with Point A configured as a pin support (Ay = +10 kN) and Point B as a roller support (By = -10 kN).

---

## Analyze

### 1. External Reactions & Internal Static Analysis
I started by creating quick hand sketches of potential truss geometries to connect all required nodes while minimizing structural weight. I selected a triangulated 5-member configuration (AB, AC, AD, BC, CD) because triangular units efficiently distribute large applied forces. I am aware that adding an extra pin in between A and B and creating 3 triangles would have created a more even distribution. However, application of the truss was not given and I wanted to create a simple truss that would be less time consuming to solve for.

Next, I solved for the external reaction forces by taking a moment equilibrium equation about Pin A, yielding vertical reaction forces of Ay = +10 kN at Pin A and By = -10 kN at Roller B. With the support reactions established, I determined internal member forces using the Method of Joints:

* **Joint B:** Summed forces in the vertical direction to set the roller reaction equal to the vertical component of Member BC, yielding the force magnitude for BC. Summing forces horizontally gave the tension force in Member AB.
* **Joint C & Joint D:** Calculated the member angles using trigonometry to resolve force components. I summed forces vertically and horizontally across Joints C and D, accounting for the applied loads of +30 kN at Joint C and -30 kN at Joint D.
* **Maximum Force:** Comparing all internal member results revealed that Member AB experiences the largest internal force of **87.58 kN in compression**.

### 2. Member Cross-Sectional Area & Frame Weight
Using the maximum compressive load, I calculated the required uniform cross-sectional area for all members:

* Applied a safety factor of 3.5 against the yield strength of A500 structural steel.
* **Symbolically solved for minimum cross-sectional area:**  
  `A_min = (F_max × FS) / σ_y`
* **Numerically evaluated the required area:**  
  `A_min = (87,580 N × 3.5) / σ_y`

To determine total frame weight, I summed the total member lengths across members (AB, AC, AD, BC, CD), multiplied by `A_min` to find the total steel volume, and applied the density of steel:

`Weight_frame = Volume × density × g`

---

### 3. Single-Shear Pin Sizing & Pin Weight
Next, I sized the connecting pins located at joints A, B, C, and D:

* **Material & Safety Factor:** Hardened tool steel with a yield shear strength `τ_y = 170 ksi` (1.1721 × 10⁹ N/m²), density `ρ_pin = 7694 kg/m³`, and a safety factor of 4.0 under single shear (`n = 1`).
* **Symbolic Pin Area:**  
  `τ_allow = τ_y / FS = V_max / A_pin  →  A_pin = (V_max × FS) / τ_y`
* **Numerical Pin Area:**  
  `A_pin = (87,580 N × 4.0) / (1.1721 × 10⁹ N/m²) = 298.9 mm²` (Radius `r = 9.755 mm`, Diameter `d = 19.51 mm`)

For a square member profile (31.1 mm × 31.1 mm cross-section giving area 967 mm²), the pin length spanning a single-shear joint between two overlapping members is `L_pin = 2 × 31.1 mm = 62.2 mm` (0.0622 m). Calculating the combined volume for all 4 pins gave a total pin weight of:

`Weight_pins = 4 × A_pin × L_pin × ρ_pin × g = 5.61 N` (0.572 kg)

---

### 4. CAD Modeling & Weight Verification
After completing hand calculations, I modeled the 3D geometry in CAD:

* Extruded the truss frame as a single part, maintaining a constant member cross-section of 967 mm² (31.1 mm × 31.1 mm square profile).
* Modeled the 4 joint pins as cylindrical parts with a diameter of 19.51 mm (`A_pin = 298.9 mm²`) and length of 62.2 mm.
* Assigned material mass densities to both parts in CAD and evaluated software Mass Properties.

---

## Decide
I selected a 5-member planar triangulated geometry because it minimizes total member count and overall structural weight while maintaining high rigidity under vertical loads. Choosing a square profile (31.1 mm × 31.1 mm) provided a uniform extrusion thickness that cleanly accommodated single-shear pin connections at each joint node.

## Communicate

### Engineering Lessons Learned
* **Yield Strength vs. External Stress Sizing:** I learned how to size structural elements by directly comparing allowable internal stress against material yield strength modified by safety factors, ensuring structural integrity without adding unnecessary material weight.
* **Joint Thickness & Pin Weight Interaction:** I discovered that pin length depends directly on beam cross-sectional geometry in single-shear joints, meaning thicker member profiles increase total pin engagement length and directly add hardware weight.
* **Hand Calculation vs. CAD Verification:** I realized the importance of computing physical mass properties by hand prior to modeling in CAD, allowing me to verify material density assignments and catch dimensional errors during software evaluation.
