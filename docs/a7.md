# Assignment 7: Finite Element Analysis (FEA) on Bracket

## Objective
The primary objective of this assignment is to understand how Finite Element Analysis (FEA) can be used as an engineering design tool to evaluate and improve a component. Rather than relying solely on theoretical calculations or assumptions, FEA allows the bracket's structural performance to be evaluated under specified loading and boundary conditions, focusing on strength, stiffness, and weight optimization.

---

## Analyze & Boundary Conditions
- **Software Used:** SOLIDWORKS Simulation (Static Study)
- **Fixtures:** Because this part supports a hanging load, a roller/slider fixture was applied to the overhangs bearing on the rail, combined with an axial restraint to prevent rigid-body translation along the rail length.
- **External Loads:** Downward force distributed along the lower cylindrical surface.

---

## Inner Loop: Mesh Convergence & Validation
Static stress analyses were run iteratively, refining the mesh to ensure results converged within a **7% difference** (or up to the maximum of three attempts) to validate that structural improvements were not numerical artifacts.

---

## Outer Loop: Design Optimization

### Design I (Baseline)
- **Description:** Initial baseline run with no geometry modifications to evaluate how hand calculations performed in simulation.
- **Results:** The bracket failed. Peak stress occurred on the cantilevered extension supporting the strap due to a large bending moment on a thin section, resulting in a Factor of Safety below 1.0.
- **Images:**
  - ![Design I Stress Plot](https://jacbinon.github.io/SOHPMORE-DESIGN/assignments/A07/)
  - ![Design I Displacement Plot](https://jacbinon.github.io/SOHPMORE-DESIGN/assignments/A07/)

### Design II (First Iteration)
- **Description:** Improved the design by thickening the strap support and adding fillets to mitigate stress concentrations at sharp right-angle intersections.
- **Results:** Improved overall strength and stiffness with lower deflection values. However, the critical location remained the cantilevered connection point due to high ongoing bending moments, and the mount still exceeded yield strength.
- **Images:**
  - ![Design II Stress Plot](https://jacbinon.github.io/SOHPMORE-DESIGN/assignments/A07/)
  - ![Design II Displacement Plot](https://jacbinon.github.io/SOHPMORE-DESIGN/assignments/A07/)

### Design III (Final Optimized Dual-Bridge)
- **Description:** Added a second symmetrical connection on the opposing side to evenly distribute the load, halved the thickness of the original side, mirrored it, and applied fillets across all remaining sharp edges.
- **Results:** Provided a substantial performance boost, achieving a passing Factor of Safety of approximately 1.36 and significantly reducing overall deflection.
- **Images:**
  - ![Design III Stress Plot](https://jacbinon.github.io/SOHPMORE-DESIGN/assignments/A07/)
  - ![Design III Displacement Plot](https://jacbinon.github.io/SOHPMORE-DESIGN/assignments/A07/)

## Communicate & Engineering Reflections

### Engineering Lessons Learned
My primary takeaway from this assignment is how significantly simplifying assumptions can alter analytical outcomes. While initial hand calculations indicated a sufficient factor of safety, FEA revealed that the baseline design would fail under the applied load. This discrepancy occurred because hand calculations assumed the pin connection experienced pure tension, neglecting the substantial bending moment caused by the load offset. Eliminating that moment arm through structural redesign highlighted how easily incorrect loading assumptions can result in a severely under-designed component.
