# A3 – Finite Element Analysis (FEA) & Design Reflection

## Objective
Conduct a Finite Element Analysis (FEA) on the aluminum bar geometry under the design tensile load, verify structural integrity against material yield strength (40 ksi), perform a design reflection comparing numerical FEA against parametric hand calculations, and evaluate stress concentration effects from a pin hole feature.

---

## Analyze

### 1. FEA Setup & Simulation Results
I assigned Aluminum Alloy (yield strength = 40 ksi) to the bar in SOLIDWORKS Simulation. A Fixed Geometry fixture was applied to the left end face, and an axial tensile force of F = 500 lbf was applied normal to the right end face. Based on hand calculations targeting a target deflection S = 0.007 in with modulus E = 8,500,000 psi, the bar was designed with a diameter D = 0.25 in (cross section A = 0.0491 in²) and extruded length L = 5.8414 in.

* **Deflection Map:** The maximum axial displacement obtained from FEA at the free end is **FEA deflection S = 0.00700 in**.
* **von Mises Stress Map:** The maximum von Mises stress across the fixed end boundary is **maximum stress = 12.94 ksi** (12,940 psi). The nominal stress away from the fixed support is **nominal stress = 10.19 ksi** (10,186 psi).
* **Safety Factor Check:** Comparing maximum von Mises stress to the material yield strength (40 ksi), the safety factor is 40 ksi divided by 12.94 ksi, which equals **3.09**.

Because maximum stress (12.94 ksi) is less than yield strength (40 ksi), resulting in a safety factor of 3.09 (greater than 1.0), the uniform bar design safely meets yield criteria under the applied 500 lbf load.

### 2. Design Reflection & Hand Calculation Comparison
Using the hand calculation equation for length, L = (deflection S * cross section A * modulus E) / force F, I solved for the required bar length L = 5.8414 in to achieve a target deflection S = 0.007 in under a 500 lbf load. I then compared the FEA deflection result against my hand calculation:

* **Hand Calculated Deflection (S):** 0.00700 in
* **FEA Deflection (S):** 0.00700 in
* **Percent Difference:** 0.00%

**Discrepancy & Model Evaluation:**
* **Agreement / Source of Difference:** The hand calculation and FEA results match with 0% error because a uniform circular cross section under pure axial tension experiences a uniform 1D stress field without geometric discontinuities. Minor localized stress concentrations exist at the fixed boundary due to restricted lateral Poisson contraction, but total axial elongation remains identical to the theoretical hand calculation.
* **Model Trust:** For a uniform bar in simple tension, I trust the hand calculation slightly more for overall axial deflection because it avoids artificial localized stress spikes introduced by rigid fixed-face boundary conditions in 3D FEA (Saint-Venant's principle).

### 3. Pin Hole Stress Concentration Analysis
To evaluate the impact of adding a pin hole (hole diameter to bar diameter ratio of 0.20, hole diameter = 0.05 in) on the left side of the bar:

* **Nominal Stress:** Away from geometric discontinuities, nominal stress in the bar is 10.19 ksi.
* **Stress Concentration Factor:** For a transverse hole in a round bar in tension (based on Peterson's Stress Concentration Factors), the factor is 2.42.
* **Estimated Peak Stress:** Multiplying the stress concentration factor of 2.42 by the nominal stress of 10.19 ksi gives a peak stress of **24.66 ksi**.
* **Safety Factor Evaluation with Hole:** Yield strength of 40 ksi divided by peak stress of 24.66 ksi gives a safety factor of **1.62**.

*Verdict:* The localized peak stress around the pin hole **PASSES** the safety factor requirement (safety factor = 1.62, which is greater than 1.0). Because peak stress (24.66 ksi) is less than yield strength (40 ksi), the material will not yield locally at the hole edges.

---

## Decide
I validated that the uniform bar safely carries the 500 lbf tensile load with a safety factor of 3.09 under standard operating conditions. Adding a pin hole feature introduces a stress concentration factor of 2.42, raising peak stress to 24.66 ksi, which still maintains a safe safety factor of 1.62 against aluminum yield strength (40 ksi).

## Communicate

### Engineering Lessons Learned
* **Boundary Condition Stiffening in FEA:** Restraining all degrees of freedom on a face prevents natural Poisson contraction, creating localized stress artifacts (12.94 ksi) near fixed supports compared to pure nominal stress (10.19 ksi).
* **Impact of Geometric Discontinuities:** Small features like pin holes dramatically amplify local stress (stress concentration factor of 2.42), demonstrating why nominal stresses alone cannot guarantee structural integrity near fasteners.
* **Verification of Hand vs. FEA:** Simple geometries should always be verified against parametric hand calculations before accepting FEA output to catch setup, unit, or meshing errors.
