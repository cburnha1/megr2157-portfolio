# A3 – Finite Element Analysis (FEA) & Design Reflection

## Objective
Conduct a Finite Element Analysis (FEA) on the aluminum bar geometry under the design tensile load, verify structural integrity against material yield strength ($S_y = 40\text{ ksi}$), perform a design reflection comparing numerical FEA against parametric hand calculations, and evaluate stress concentration effects ($K_t$) from a pin hole feature.

---

## Analyze

### 1. FEA Setup & Simulation Results
I assigned Aluminum Alloy ($S_y = 40\text{ ksi}$) to the bar model in SOLIDWORKS Simulation. A Fixed Geometry fixture was applied to the left end face, and an axial tensile force of $F = 500\text{ lbf}$ was applied normal to the right end face. The bar diameter is $D = 0.25\text{ in}$ ($A = 0.0491\text{ in}^2$) and length $L = 5.8414\text{ in}$.

* **Deflection Map:** The maximum axial displacement obtained from FEA at the free end is **$\delta_{\text{FEA}} = 0.00700\text{ in}$**.
* **von Mises Stress Map:** The maximum von Mises stress across the fixed end boundary is **$\sigma_{\text{max}} = 12.94\text{ ksi}$** ($12,940\text{ psi}$). The nominal stress away from the fixed support is **$\sigma_{\text{nom}} = 10.19\text{ ksi}$** ($10,186\text{ psi}$).
* **Safety Factor Check:** Comparing maximum von Mises stress to the material yield strength ($S_y = 40\text{ ksi}$):

$$\text{FS} = \frac{S_y}{\sigma_{\text{max}}} = \frac{40\text{ ksi}}{12.94\text{ ksi}} = 3.09$$

Because $\sigma_{\text{max}} = 12.94\text{ ksi} < 40\text{ ksi}$ ($\text{FS} = 3.09 > 1.0$), the uniform bar design safely meets yield criteria under the applied $500\text{ lbf}$ load.

### 2. Design Reflection & Hand Calculation Comparison
I compared the FEA deflection result against my parametric hand calculation ($\delta_{\text{hand}} = \frac{FL}{AE}$):

* **Hand Calculated Deflection:** $\delta_{\text{hand}} = 0.00700\text{ in}$
* **FEA Deflection:** $\delta_{\text{FEA}} = 0.00700\text{ in}$
* **Percent Difference:**

$$\text{Percent Difference} = \frac{|0.00700 - 0.00700|}{0.00700} \times 100\% = 0.00\%$$

**Discrepancy & Model Evaluation:**
* **Agreement / Source of Difference:** The hand calculation and FEA results match with 0% error because a uniform circular cross-section under pure axial tension experiences a uniform 1D stress field ($\sigma = \frac{P}{A}$) without geometric discontinuities. Minor localized stress concentrations exist at the fixed boundary due to restricted lateral Poisson contraction ($\nu \approx 0.33$), but total axial elongation remains identical to the 1D theoretical bar equation.
* **Model Trust:** For a uniform prismatic bar in simple tension, I trust the 1D parametric hand calculation ($\frac{FL}{AE}$) slightly more for overall axial deflection because it avoids artificial localized stress spikes introduced by rigid fixed-face boundary conditions in 3D FEA (Saint-Venant's principle).

### 3. Pin Hole Stress Concentration Analysis ($K_t$)
To evaluate the impact of adding a substantial pin hole ($d/D \approx 0.20$, $d = 0.05\text{ in}$) on the left side of the bar:

* **Nominal Stress ($\sigma_{\text{nom}}$):** Away from geometric discontinuities, the nominal stress in the bar is $\sigma_{\text{nom}} = 10.19\text{ ksi}$.
* **Stress Concentration Factor ($K_t$):** For a transverse hole in a round bar in tension (Peterson's Stress Concentration Factors), $K_t \approx 2.42$.
* **Estimated Peak Stress:**

$$\sigma_{\text{peak}} = K_t \times \sigma_{\text{nom}} = 2.42 \times 10.19\text{ ksi} = 24.66\text{ ksi}$$

* **Safety Factor Evaluation with Hole:**

$$\text{FS}_{\text{hole}} = \frac{S_y}{\sigma_{\text{peak}}} = \frac{40\text{ ksi}}{24.66\text{ ksi}} = 1.62$$

*Verdict:* The localized peak stress around the pin hole **PASSES** the safety factor requirement ($\text{FS}_{\text{hole}} = 1.62 \ge 1.0$). Because $\sigma_{\text{peak}} = 24.66\text{ ksi} < 40\text{ ksi}$, the material will not yield locally at the hole edges.

---

## Decide
I validated that the uniform bar safely carries the $500\text{ lbf}$ tensile load with a safety factor of 3.09 under standard operating conditions. Adding a pin hole feature introduces a stress concentration factor ($K_t = 2.42$), raising the peak stress to $24.66\text{ ksi}$, which still maintains a safe factor of safety ($\text{FS} = 1.62$) against aluminum yield strength ($40\text{ ksi}$).

---

## Communicate

### Engineering Lessons Learned
* **Boundary Condition Stiffening in FEA:** Restraining all degrees of freedom on a 3D face prevents natural Poisson contraction, creating localized stress artifacts ($12.94\text{ ksi}$) near fixed supports compared to pure nominal stress ($10.19\text{ ksi}$).
* **Impact of Geometric Discontinuities ($K_t$):** Small features like pin holes dramatically amplify local stress ($K_t \approx 2.42$), demonstrating why nominal stresses alone cannot guarantee structural integrity near fasteners.
* **Verification of Hand vs. FEA:** Simple geometries should always be verified against parametric hand calculations before accepting FEA output to catch setup, unit, or meshing errors.
