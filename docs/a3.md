# A3 – Finite Element Analysis (FEA) & Design Reflection

## Objective
Conduct a Finite Element Analysis (FEA) on the aluminum bar geometry under the design tensile load, verify structural integrity against material yield strength (Sy = 40 ksi), perform a design reflection comparing numerical FEA against parametric hand calculations, and evaluate stress concentration effects (Kt) from a pin hole feature.

---

## Analyze

### 1. FEA Setup & Simulation Results
I assigned Aluminum Alloy (Sy = 40 ksi) to the bar in SOLIDWORKS Simulation. A Fixed Geometry fixture was applied to the left end face, and an axial tensile force of F = 500 lbf was applied normal to the right end face. Based on hand calculations targeting a 0.007 in deflection with E = 8.5 * 10^6 psi, the bar was designed with a diameter of D = 0.25 in (Area = 0.0491 in²) and extruded length L = 5.8414 in.

* **Deflection Map:** The maximum axial displacement obtained from FEA at the free end is **delta_FEA = 0.00700 in**.
* **von Mises Stress Map:** The maximum von Mises stress across the fixed end boundary is **sigma_max = 12.94 ksi** (12,940 psi). The nominal stress away from the fixed support is **sigma_nom = 10.19 ksi** (10,186 psi).
* **Safety Factor Check:** Comparing maximum von Mises stress to the material yield strength (Sy = 40 ksi):

FS = Sy / sigma_max = 40 ksi / 12.94 ksi = 3.09

Because sigma_max = 12.94 ksi < 40 ksi (FS = 3.09 > 1.0), the uniform bar design safely meets yield criteria under the applied 500 lbf load.

### 2. Design Reflection & Hand Calculation Comparison
Using the parametric equation L = (delta * A * E) / F, I solved for the required bar length L = 5.8414 in to achieve a target deflection of 0.007 in under a 500 lbf load. I then compared the FEA deflection result against my hand calculation:

* **Hand Calculated Deflection:** delta_hand = 0.00700 in
* **FEA Deflection:** delta_FEA = 0.00700 in
* **Percent Difference:**

Percent Difference = (|0.00700 - 0.00700| / 0.00700) * 100% = 0.00%

**Discrepancy & Model Evaluation:**
* **Agreement / Source of Difference:** The hand calculation and FEA results match with 0% error because a uniform circular cross-section under pure axial tension experiences a uniform 1D stress field (sigma = P / A) without geometric discontinuities. Minor localized stress concentrations exist at the fixed boundary due to restricted lateral Poisson contraction (v ≈ 0.33), but total axial elongation remains identical to the 1D theoretical bar equation.
* **Model Trust:** For a uniform bar in simple tension, I trust the 1D parametric hand calculation (FL / AE) slightly more for overall axial deflection because it avoids artificial localized stress spikes introduced by rigid fixed-face boundary conditions in 3D FEA (Saint-Venant's principle).

### 3. Pin Hole Stress Concentration Analysis (Kt)
To evaluate the impact of adding a substantial pin hole (d/D ≈ 0.20, d = 0.05 in) on the left side of the bar:

* **Nominal Stress (sigma_nom):** Away from geometric discontinuities, the nominal stress in the bar is sigma_nom = 10.19 ksi.
* **Stress Concentration Factor (Kt):** For a transverse hole in a round bar in tension (Peterson's Stress Concentration Factors), Kt = 2.42.
* **Estimated Peak Stress:**

sigma_peak = Kt * sigma_nom = 2.42 * 10.19 ksi = 24.66 ksi

* **Safety Factor Evaluation with Hole:**

FS_hole = Sy / sigma_peak = 40 ksi / 24.66 ksi = 1.62

*Verdict:* The localized peak stress around the pin hole **PASSES** the safety factor requirement (FS_hole = 1.62 >= 1.0). Because sigma_peak = 24.66 ksi < 40 ksi, the material will not yield locally at the hole edges.

---

## Decide
I validated that the uniform bar safely carries the 500 lbf tensile load with a safety factor of 3.09 under standard operating conditions. Adding a pin hole feature introduces a stress concentration factor (Kt = 2.42), raising the peak stress to 24.66 ksi, which still maintains a safe factor of safety (FS = 1.62) against aluminum yield strength (40 ksi).

## Communicate

### Engineering Lessons Learned
* **Boundary Condition Stiffening in FEA:** Restraining all degrees of freedom on a face prevents natural Poisson contraction, creating localized stress artifacts (12.94 ksi) near fixed supports compared to pure nominal stress (10.19 ksi).
* **Impact of Geometric Discontinuities (Kt):** Small features like pin holes dramatically amplify local stress (Kt ≈ 2.42), demonstrating why nominal stresses alone cannot guarantee structural integrity near fasteners.
* **Verification of Hand vs. FEA:** Simple geometries should always be verified against parametric hand calculations before accepting FEA output to catch setup, unit, or meshing errors.
