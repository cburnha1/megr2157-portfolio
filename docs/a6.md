## Decide

### Parametric CAD Modeling
The bracket was modeled in SolidWorks using global variables to drive the critical feature dimensions directly from analytical formulas rather than hardcoded values. 

* **Global Variable Link:** The top flange thickness and sliding gap clearance were declared as global parameters.
* **Design Considerations:** Added internal fillets at sharp corners to minimize stress concentrations and improve manufacturability.

### CAD Drawing & Tolerancing
Using the 3D model, a 3-view engineering drawing was created using **Third Angle Projection**. Dimensions were added to define all features required for manufacturing.

* **Sliding Fit Tolerances:** Applied tighter tolerances ($\pm 0.005\text{ in}$) across the gap and mating channels to ensure proper sliding fit over the rigid T-beam.
* **General Features:** Applied standard title block tolerances ($\pm 0.010\text{ in}$ / $\pm 0.020\text{ in}$) to non-mating dimensions.

## Communicate

### Engineering Reflections

#### Parametric Equation Integration
The dimension for the top flange thickness ($0.79\text{ in}$) was driven directly by the bending strength equation programmed into SolidWorks global variables (`"t_flange" = 0.79`). Because this parameter was linked directly to the feature depth in the sketch, any updates to the strength calculations automatically propagate through the feature tree, updating downstream geometry without requiring manual sketch edits or repairing broken mates.

#### Tolerance Selection & Functional Justification
* **Tight Tolerance ($\pm 0.005\text{ in}$):** Applied specifically to the interior sliding channels and gap mating with the T-beam. These are functional mating surfaces where tight control is mandatory to maintain a proper sliding fit without excessive play or mechanical binding.
* **Loose Tolerance ($\pm 0.020\text{ in}$):** Applied to external non-mating boundaries, such as outer edge lengths and fillet radii. Because these features do not interface with other components, looser tolerances reduce machining cycle times, eliminate needless precision grinding, and lower overall fabrication costs.
* **Cost Impact of Universal Tight Tolerances:** Applying a $\pm 0.005\text{ in}$ tolerance universally across non-critical features unnecessarily inflates production costs by requiring slower machine feed rates, higher precision tooling, stricter environmental controls, and higher scrap rates during quality inspection without providing any functional benefit to the bracket assembly.
